## Facts (all verified, March 2026)
1. Mar 2026, Haidilao hot pot, Cupertino, California: a humanoid robot dancing in the dining room got too close to a table, struck it, and sent plates, chopsticks and condiments flying. — TechCrunch 2026-03-19; NBC News; Fox Business
2. It was in "celebration mode", a built-in routine for birthdays and special events. — Business Insider via Ground News; Pulse 2026-03-20
3. The restaurant said a guest asked for it to be brought closer to the table, which is not its normal setting, and the limited space affected its movement. — Fox Business / NBC statement
4. The restaurant said it was "not malfunctioning or out of control"; the moves are pre-programmed. — NBC News; eWeek
5. At least three staff struggled to restrain it; it kept dancing. — Interesting Engineering
6. One employee looked at her phone, possibly trying to reach the off switch through an app (TechCrunch's guess, so the script says "possibly"). — TechCrunch
7. It kept going for about another minute with a sauce-stained hand and a cheerful face on its screen. — Fox Business
8. Pots of hot broth were on the tables. No injuries were reported and no broth spill was reported. — Interesting Engineering; LatestLY
9. The robot appears to be an AgiBot X2. Not named on screen, and the robot design is our own. — TechCrunch

The everyday analogy is a wind-up toy marching off a counter edge. The idea it explains is open-loop control: moves played back from a plan, without sensing what's in the way.

## Voice-over (one steady deadpan tone)

**HOOK**
In March, a robot in Silicon Valley was asked to dance. It destroyed a dinner table.

**SETTING**
Cupertino, California. A hot pot restaurant, proud of its robots. This one wore an apron, and for birthdays it had a special setting: celebration mode.

**ACTION**
A guest asked for it to come a little closer to their table.
So it did.
The music started. Arms up.
Arms out.

**SNAP**
Arms — straight down onto the table.

**ACTION (escalation)**
Chopsticks took off. Sauces took off. Plates took off.
Three waiters grabbed hold of it. It kept dancing.
One of them checked her phone, possibly looking for the off switch.

**DEADPAN DOUBLE**
Luckily, the pots of boiling broth stayed put.
Unluckily, the robot kept going for about another minute — sauce on its hand, smile on its face.

**EXPLANATION**
Here's the thing. The robot wasn't watching the table. Its dance was a plan: every move played back at full swing, whatever was in the way.
Like a wind-up toy marching across the kitchen counter. It doesn't know there's an edge. It only knows: left foot, right foot.

**CONSEQUENCE**
The restaurant said the robot was not malfunctioning, or out of control. It did exactly what it was programmed to do. Just in a smaller space than usual.

**CALLBACK TWIST**
And that's the thing about a wind-up toy.
It doesn't stop at the edge.
It stops when the song does.
